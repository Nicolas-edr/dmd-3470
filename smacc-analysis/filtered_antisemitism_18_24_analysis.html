Key shifts — what filtering reveals for 18–24s

Micro-accounts dominate filtered reach. uh1269 has just 471 followers but holds 33% share of voice in the filtered set — reaching 1.3M people. f2.memo has only 44 followers but reached 378K. This is the clearest evidence of algorithmic amplification targeting young feeds.
TikTok's positive skew drops when filtered. In the full dataset TikTok showed 29% positive. In the filtered set it falls to 20% positive, and negative rises to 23%. The filtered content is more hostile — the broader set was diluted with softer solidarity posts.
News sources cluster around Jewish press. The top links all point to jpost.com (Jerusalem Post). This suggests the news layer is largely reactive/reporting coverage, not origination — but it still adds volume and legitimacy to the topic in search.
PragerU and Destiny both appear. These are ideologically opposing YouTube channels with massive young-adult audiences. Their presence in the filtered set shows this content actively enters the 18–24 debate/commentary ecosystem from multiple angles.
LinkedIn at scale is new. 94 mentions on LinkedIn ties X — in the filtered set this matters. It indicates professional, institutional, and campus-level discussion (student unions, university departments, employers) is a parallel channel, not just street-level social media.

What filtering tells us about the reach mechanism

The "noise" layer (87% of full set)
Solidarity posts, viral tags (#viral, #fyp, #iran), and geopolitical content that mentions antisemitism tangentially. High reach (75M), softer framing. Primary exposure layer for passive 18–24 audiences.
The "signal" layer (13% — this filtered set)
Direct content using #antisemitism, #jewish, specific incident links. Smaller reach (24M) but more explicit. More negative sentiment. This is where ideological formation happens.
How they connect for 18–24s
The noise layer creates passive familiarity via TikTok feeds. The signal layer converts engaged viewers into active consumers. #fyp surviving in both layers confirms the algorithm bridges the two.
What's missing from both
Telegram (private channels), Instagram (Meta API gap), coded/dog-whistle language, and comment-section content. The true exposure floor is higher than either dataset shows.

Cross-period trend: Both current and prior periods show remarkably stable sentiment ratios — 24% negative, 5% positive in both filtered periods. This consistency across two 15-day windows suggests this is not a spike event but a sustained, structural pattern of content reaching young audiences.
